Feeding a large senior dog from a bowl on the floor can mean a lot of bending and stretching that just isn't comfortable anymore, especially for dogs with slower, stiffer movements. Raised dog bowls bring food and water up to a more natural height, and they also cut down on the sliding, spilling, and chasing that bowls on hard floors tend to cause. The right stand depends on your dog's size, how high you need the bowls to sit, and how much mess control you want built in.

## What Height and Width Does Your Dog Need?

Raised feeders work best when the bowl rim sits close to your dog's elbow, and the stand is wide enough to hold their bowls steady. Here's how to check both in about two minutes.

**Step 1: Measure your dog's elbow height.** With your dog standing normally, measure straight up from the floor to the point of the elbow. Note it in inches.

**Step 2: Check your dog's shoulder height too.** Some listings reference shoulder height as a general size cue rather than exact feeder height, so measure from the floor to the top of the shoulder blades as well.

**Step 3: Measure the bowls you plan to use.** Measure the width of each bowl at its widest point, since the stand needs an opening that matches.

| Dog's elbow height | A stand height to look for |
|---|---|
| 12 in (30 cm) | Around 10–12 in |
| 14 in (36 cm) | Around 12–14 in |
| 16 in (41 cm) | Around 14–16 in |
| 18 in (46 cm) | Around 16 in and up |

## How to Choose a Raised Dog Bowl Stand

- **Height range**: Match the stand's height options to your dog's shoulder height — some stands top out around 12 inches, while others adjust up to 16 inches.
- **Adjustability**: If your dog is still growing or you want flexibility, look for stands with multiple height settings rather than a fixed height.
- **Bowl width compatibility**: Check whether the stand is designed for a specific bowl size or can adjust to fit different bowl widths.
- **Number of bowls**: Decide if you need one bowl, two bowls for food and water, or a stand that includes both.
- **Material**: Stands come in wood, bamboo, and metal; pick based on the look you want and how it fits your space.
- **Mess control features**: If your dog tends to splash water or scatter kibble, a design with a spill-catching tray or an included mat can help keep the area tidy.
- **Cleaning needs**: Dishwasher-safe bowls or stands make upkeep easier if you prefer not to hand-wash after every meal.
- **Assembly**: Tool-free or hardware-included assembly can matter if you want to set up the stand quickly.
- **Household size**: For multi-dog homes, a stand listed for a range of sizes can serve more than one dog comfortably.
