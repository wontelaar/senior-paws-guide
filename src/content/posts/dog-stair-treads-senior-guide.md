When a senior dog starts hesitating at the top of the stairs or slipping on the way down, it's natural to start searching for an easy fix. Non-slip stair treads are one of the most common solutions people try first, and this FAQ walks through how they work, what to look for, and how to match a set to your staircase.

### How do stair treads help my older dog on wooden stairs?

Treads add grip to an otherwise slick wooden or laminate surface, which makes a staircase easier for a dog to navigate confidently. They're a simple, low-effort way to build a safer home for a senior dog, and they pair nicely with a baby gate at the top or bottom of the stairs for extra peace of mind. If a dog has significant mobility problems, repeated falls, or seems disoriented on stairs, mention it to your vet.

### What's the best way to add grip to my stairs?

Stair treads designed to adhere to each individual step are a more secure fit than a rug runner or loose mat, which can shift under a dog's paws. Because each tread is applied step by step rather than running the full length of the staircase, every step gets its own dependable footing. ### What else can make the stairs safer for my senior dog?

Treads are an easy first step for traction, and they pair well with other helpful additions: a baby gate at the top or bottom of the stairs, a ramp, or carrying your dog on the stairs when you're nearby. Starting with treads on every step your dog uses gives you a solid base to build on, and the low effort of a peel-and-stick install means your home can be ready quickly.
