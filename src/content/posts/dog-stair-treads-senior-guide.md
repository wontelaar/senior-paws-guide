When a senior dog starts hesitating at the top of the stairs or slipping on the way down, it's natural to start searching for an easy fix. Non-slip stair treads are one of the most common solutions people try first, and this FAQ walks through what they can realistically do, what to look for, and where they fall short.

### Will stair treads actually stop my older dog from falling?

Treads add grip to an otherwise slick wooden or laminate surface, which can make a staircase easier for a dog to navigate confidently, but they aren't a guarantee against falls. A senior dog that struggles on stairs may be dealing with arthritis, declining vision, vestibular issues, or general muscle weakness, and treads don't address any of those underlying causes. If a dog has significant mobility problems, repeated falls, or seems disoriented on stairs, it's worth talking to a vet about whether the stairs should be blocked off entirely with a gate, or whether the dog should be carried up and down instead. Treads are best thought of as one part of a safer environment, not a fix for the dog's physical condition.

### Can't I just use a rug runner or yoga mat instead of actual stair treads?

A rug runner or loose mat can shift, bunch, or slide out from under a dog's paws, which is exactly the hazard you're trying to eliminate on stairs. ### What else should I do besides adding stair treads?

Since treads only address traction and not the reason a senior dog is struggling, pair them with a broader conversation with your vet about the dog's mobility, joint health, and vision. Depending on what the vet finds, that conversation might lead to a baby gate at the top or bottom of the stairs, a ramp, assistance carrying the dog on stairs altogether, or treatment for an underlying condition. Stair treads can be a reasonable, low-effort first step in making the home safer, but they work best as one piece of a larger plan rather than the whole solution.

Choosing the right stair treads comes down to matching the pack size and dimensions to your own staircase, checking in on the adhesive over time, and treating the product as a traction aid rather than a cure for whatever's making your dog unsteady. A quick conversation with your vet can help you figure out whether treads alone are enough, or whether your senior dog needs more support getting up and down the stairs safely.
