An older dog that's already stiff or arthritic may not tolerate a lot of handling, so a quick, soothing spray is often a more practical first step than a full bath or ointment that requires rubbing into tender skin. This guide compares four widely used hot spot and itch relief sprays to help narrow down which fits an aging dog's needs.

## FAQ

**Can these sprays be used on open wounds or broken skin?**
These products are intended for topical use on intact, irritated skin — not on open wounds, near the eyes, or around the mouth. If a hot spot is oozing, spreading, or looks like broken or raw skin, it's best to have a vet examine it rather than apply a spray repeatedly at home.

**How do I know if a hot spot needs a vet visit instead of home treatment?**
A hot spot that keeps spreading, isn't improving after a few days of care, or comes with hair loss, a strong odor, or signs that the dog is in pain warrants a vet exam. This is especially true for senior dogs, since underlying issues like infections or allergies may need more than a topical spray to resolve.

**Is it safe to use a hydrocortisone spray on a senior dog long-term?**
Hydrocortisone sprays, such as Pet MD's 1% formula <a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B083781BSY?tag=seniorpawsgui-20" target="_blank" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">Pet MD Hydrocortisone Spray (4 oz)</a>, are generally meant for short-term relief rather than continuous, ongoing use. If a senior dog has recurring itching or skin irritation, it's worth discussing a longer-term plan with a vet instead of relying on repeated hydrocortisone applications.
