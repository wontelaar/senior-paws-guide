As dogs get older, their joints and muscles need more cushioning than a thin pad or hand-me-down couch cushion can offer, especially for large breeds carrying more weight on every pressure point. A supportive, appropriately sized bed can make the difference between a dog that rests comfortably through the night and one that struggles to get settled. This guide compares four orthopedic picks built with memory foam or egg-crate foam, so you can match the right size, cover, and foam style to your dog's needs.

## What Size Does Your Dog (and Bed Space) Need?

Picking the right orthopedic bed is mostly about matching your dog's body length to the bed's sleeping surface. Here is a two-minute way to check it.

**Step 1: Measure your dog.** With your dog standing, measure from the tip of the nose to the base of the tail, then add the length of their legs if they like to stretch out fully when sleeping. Note the result in inches.

**Step 2: Measure your dog's weight.** Use a bathroom scale (weigh yourself holding your dog, then subtract your own weight) and round to the nearest pound.

**Step 3: Compare to the bed's listed footprint.** Check the bed's length and width against your dog's nose-to-tail measurement, leaving a few inches of margin so they can curl up or stretch without hanging off the edge.

## How to Choose

- **Dog's weight and size:** Match the bed's listed weight range and dimensions to your dog rather than sizing up or down, since orthopedic beds are designed around specific weight brackets.
- **Foam type:** Egg-crate foam and memory foam offer different surface feels; some beds, like the OhGeni, combine both in a reversible format so your dog can choose.
- **Cover washability:** Look for a machine-washable, zippered cover if you want easy cleanup, and keep in mind that the foam itself is often not washable even when the cover is.
- **Waterproof liners:** If your dog is prone to accidents or you want extra protection for the foam core, a bed with a waterproof inner layer or lining, such as the KSIIA or OhGeni, can be a practical choice.
- **Bottom grip:** A non-skid or nonskid base helps keep the bed from sliding on hardwood or tile floors.
- **Bed thickness:** Thicker foam, such as the 3.5-inch or 3-inch options here, provides a deeper bed profile, which some owners prefer for larger dogs.
- **Fabric preference:** Sherpa tops offer a plush feel, while two-sided designs with Oxford fabric give you a more rugged surface option for certain seasons or environments.

## FAQ

**How do I know what size orthopedic bed my dog needs?**
Check your dog's weight against the bed's listed weight range and compare your dog's length to the bed's dimensions so they can lie fully stretched out. Each listing above specifies the weight range and bed measurements it's designed for.

**Are these beds machine washable?**
The covers on each of these beds are designed to be removable and machine washable via a zipper closure, though the foam inserts themselves are generally not washable. Always follow the specific care instructions on the listing for best results.

**Should I ask my vet before choosing a bed for an older dog?**
If your dog has a diagnosed health condition or mobility concerns, it's a good idea to ask your vet about what kind of bedding setup suits their specific needs.
