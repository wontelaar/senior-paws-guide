## How to Choose

- **Format preference**: Chews (VetriScience, Zesty Paws, CardioMAX) work well for dogs that take treats readily, while a liquid like Pet Wellbeing's suits dogs that resist pills or chews and owners comfortable mixing supplements into food.
- **Ingredient breadth**: Some products focus on a core trio (taurine, CoQ10, carnitine), while others add hawthorn berry and omega-3 fish oil. Pick the core trio for a straightforward formula, or a broader blend for a fuller ingredient list in one chew.
- **Flavor**: Chicken and salmon flavors are both represented here; dog preference and any known food sensitivities may guide this choice.
- **Manufacturing transparency**: If certification matters to you, check listings for details like NASC certification, which CardioMAX's listing mentions for its US manufacturing facility.

## FAQ

**Can I give a heart supplement alongside my dog's regular care?**
Yes — many owners add one to a vet-directed routine. The supplements on this list are marketed for general cardiovascular support, and the chews are simple to work into a daily routine as a treat.

**Which ingredients should I look for in a heart supplement?**
Taurine, L-carnitine, and CoQ10 show up across the chews in this guide, while Zesty Paws and CardioMAX also add hawthorn and omega-3s. Choose the formula whose ingredient list matches what you want for your dog, whether that's a focused trio or a fuller blend.

**How do I decide between a chew and a liquid formula?**
It often comes down to your dog's eating habits and your routine — chews are easy to give as treats, while liquids mixed into food can work for dogs that are picky about chews or pills. The comparison table above shows each format at a glance, so you can pick the one that suits your household.
