This guide compares four popular omega-3 options so you can match the practical features of each to your dog.

## How to Choose

- **Dog's size and weight**: Each label gives weight-based serving guidance, so match it to your dog and plan how quickly a bottle or bag will be used up, especially for larger senior dogs.
- **Format preference**: Liquids mix invisibly into food and suit dogs who are suspicious of new treats, while chews work better for dogs who enjoy a snack-time ritual or who refuse doctored food.
- **Your dog's current routine**: If your dog is on a prescription diet or medication, mention a new fish oil supplement to your veterinarian before starting.
- **Ingredient simplicity vs. added extras**: Some owners want a pure oil with minimal ingredients, while others like the added vitamins or biotin bundled into a chew — decide which matters more for your dog's needs.
- **Ease of storage and handling**: Pump bottles pour right over the meal, while chews are simple to portion out and easy to take along.
- **Budget and quantity**: Compare how many servings each product provides relative to your dog's size so you can judge value over time rather than judging by price alone.

## FAQ

**What are these fish oils marketed for?**
Many fish oil products are marketed with claims around joint comfort, mobility, and skin-and-coat support, so you can choose the format and formula that match what matters most for your dog.

**Should I choose a liquid or a chew for my older dog?**
It depends on your dog's eating habits. Liquids offer flexibility for mixing into food, chews work well for dogs who respond better to treat-like items, and soft gels like Nordic Naturals give a neat, fixed serving.

**How do I serve fish oil to my senior dog?**
Follow the label instructions for the product you choose, since serving size varies by product, concentration, and your dog's weight. Pump bottles pour over a regular meal, soft gels can go in food, and chews are given like treats.
