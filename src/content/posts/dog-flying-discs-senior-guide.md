A dog that's slower to get up or a little stiff in the joints can still chase and catch happily when the throw is light, soft, and easy on the mouth. A flying disc made of pliable rubber or soft nylon offers a gentler alternative to a stick or a hard ball, giving an older or arthritic dog a toy that's easy to grab without a jarring impact. This guide compares four popular dog frisbees by material, size, and intended use so you can match one to your dog's size and play style.

## What Size Disc Does Your Dog Need?

Disc size and material decide how easily your dog can grip, carry, and catch a throw. A quick check of your dog's weight and jaw width helps you match them to the right disc.

**Step 1: Weigh your dog.** Use a home scale (step on holding your dog, then subtract your own weight) or check their last vet visit record.

**Step 2: Check your dog's mouth width.** Gently measure the width across your dog's open mouth, or simply note whether they're generally described as a small, medium, or large breed.

**Step 3: Match the disc diameter to their size.** Smaller dogs generally do better gripping smaller, lighter discs, while medium and large dogs can usually manage wider discs. If your dog has any jaw, dental, or joint sensitivity, check with your vet before starting disc play.

**Disc diameter at a glance**

## How to Choose

- **Dog size:** Match the disc size to your dog's build — the KONG Flyer is sized for small dogs, while the Nerf Dog Atomic Flyer is intended for medium and large dogs, and the Chuckit! Paraflight comes in a large size for bigger dogs.
- **Material feel:** Rubber discs like the Nerf Dog Atomic Flyer and KONG Flyer offer a firmer feel, while nylon options like the Hyper Pet Flippy Flopper are softer and lighter — consider which your dog tends to prefer when carrying toys.
- **Floating ability:** If play happens near water, a disc that floats, such as the Chuckit! Paraflight or Hyper Pet Flippy Flopper, makes retrieves from a pond or pool easier to manage.
- **Weight:** Lighter discs, like the Hyper Pet Flippy Flopper at 2.88 oz or the KONG Flyer at 4.23 oz, may be easier for smaller or less powerful jaws to carry, while the heavier Nerf Dog Atomic Flyer at 6.74 oz suits a bigger dog's grip.
- **Supervision needs:** Some discs, like the Hyper Pet Flippy Flopper, are noted as not intended for chewing, so plan to supervise play and swap in a chew toy for solo downtime.
- **Weather conditions:** If play often happens outdoors in wet or variable weather, a weather-resistant option like the Nerf Dog Atomic Flyer is worth considering.

## FAQ

**Are dog frisbees safe for older or arthritic dogs to play with?**
Soft nylon or pliable rubber discs are generally gentler than hard balls or sticks since they give a little on contact, but every dog's needs are different, so check with your vet if your dog has a joint condition before starting a new activity.

**Do all dog frisbees float?**
Not necessarily — the Chuckit! Paraflight and Hyper Pet Flippy Flopper are both listed as floating discs, which makes them convenient for water play, while the Nerf Dog Atomic Flyer is described as waterproof and weather-resistant. Check each listing for its specific water-related features before buying for lake or pool use.

**What size disc is right for my dog?**
It depends on your dog's size and how they like to play — smaller dogs tend to do well with a compact disc like the 7-inch KONG Flyer, while medium and large dogs have more to work with from the 10-inch Nerf Dog Atomic Flyer or the large-sized Chuckit! Paraflight.
