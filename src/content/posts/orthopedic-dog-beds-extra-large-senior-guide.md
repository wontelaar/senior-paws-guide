## What Size Bed Does Your Dog Need?

Extra-large senior dogs need a bed long enough to lie flat and wide enough to stretch out without hanging off the edge. Here is how to match your dog to the right bed in about two minutes.

**Step 1: Measure your dog.** With your dog lying on their side in a relaxed, fully stretched position, measure from the tip of the nose to the base of the tail. Then measure their height from the floor to the top of the shoulder while standing.

**Step 2: Add a buffer.** A good bed length is your dog's nose-to-tail measurement plus 6 to 8 inches, so they can stretch out fully and shift positions without an edge of the bed in the way.

**Step 3: Check the bed's listed length against your dog's weight and length.** Match your dog's weight to the weight limits below, then confirm the bed's listed length covers your dog's stretched-out length plus buffer.

| Dog's stretched length | Recommended bed length (plus buffer) |
|---|---|
| 36 in | 42-44 in |
| 40 in | 46-48 in |
| 44 in | 50-52 in |
| 48 in | 54-56 in |

## How to Choose

- **Weight and size listing**: Check that the bed is rated for your dog's actual weight — some of these options are listed up to 150 lb, which matters for giant breeds.
- **Bed dimensions vs. your dog's stretched-out length**: Measure your dog lying fully stretched out and compare against the bed's listed length and width so they have room to move and resettle.
- **Foam type and certification**: Memory foam provides a cushioned, supportive surface; certifications like CertiPUR-US or OEKO-TEX can offer added assurance about foam content and fabric safety if that matters to you.
- **Waterproof liner**: A TPU waterproof liner helps protect the foam core from accidents, drool, or spills, which is especially useful for senior dogs.
- **Washable cover**: A removable, machine-washable cover makes regular cleaning far simpler, particularly for dogs that shed or track in dirt.
- **Low entry height**: A low-profile design can make it easier for a senior or less mobile dog to get on and off the bed.
- **Non-slip bottom**: This keeps the bed from sliding around on hardwood, tile, or laminate floors.
- **Bolsters vs. flat design**: Bolstered sides give a dog something to rest their head against, while a flat design offers an open surface — consider your dog's sleeping habits.
- **Expandable or multi-piece options**: If you have more than one large dog or want extra space, a two-piece set that joins together can be a practical solution.
- **Crate compatibility**: If you use a crate, a bed or cushion sized to fit it (such as 42 or 48 inch crates) adds convenience.

## FAQ

**How do I know if a bed is really big enough for a 100+ pound dog?**
Measure your dog from nose to tail base while lying down and add a few inches, then compare that to the bed's listed length and width. Beds listed for up to 150 lb, like the Sytopia XXL or the 2-Piece 54-Inch bed with bolsters, are designed with extra-large and giant breeds in mind.

**Will an orthopedic bed help with my dog's joint pain?**
These beds are made with firm, supportive, or cushioned foam rather than being medical devices, so it's best to ask your vet about any specific joint or mobility concerns your dog has. A low-entry design can simply make it easier for a senior dog to get on and off the bed.

**Can these beds handle accidents or drooling?**
Several of these options include a waterproof TPU liner under a removable, machine-washable cover, which makes cleanup straightforward. Always follow the care instructions on the specific listing to keep the cover and foam in good shape.
