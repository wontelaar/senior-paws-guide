As dogs age, digestive regularity often gets less predictable — arthritis and reduced activity can slow things down, medications can upset stool consistency, and a senior gut simply doesn't process food the way it did at two years old. A pumpkin or fiber supplement can be a simple, low-effort way to support day-to-day regularity, but it's worth understanding what these products actually are (mostly fiber, sometimes with added enzymes) before picking one off a shelf. Below is a comparison of four widely used options to help narrow down what fits your dog's routine.

## How to Choose

- **Format preference**: Canned puree, powder, or chew — think about what your senior dog will actually eat without a fight, and what's easiest for you to store and portion.
- **Size and weight of your dog**: Chews like Glandex are dosed by weight, so make sure you check the listing for weight-based guidance rather than guessing.
- **Ingredient simplicity**: If you want to avoid fillers or prefer organic sourcing, look closely at ingredient lists — some products list just two or three ingredients, while others include a broader blend.
- **Specific concern vs. general regularity**: A general pumpkin or fiber topper may be enough for occasional firmness support, but if scooting or anal gland issues are part of the picture, a formula that includes digestive enzymes may be more relevant.
- **Storage and shelf life**: Canned products typically need refrigeration after opening, while powders and chews in resealable packaging may be easier to keep on hand long-term.
- **Budget**: Prices and package sizes vary, so compare cost per serving on the listing rather than assuming one format is automatically more economical.
- **Your dog's actual response**: Every dog's gut reacts differently to added fiber — some may need less than expected, others more — so introduce gradually and watch for changes.

## FAQ

**Can pumpkin or fiber supplements fix chronic diarrhea or constipation in senior dogs?**
No. These products are designed to support day-to-day digestive regularity, not to diagnose or treat an underlying medical cause. If a senior dog has new, worsening, or persistent digestive symptoms, that warrants a veterinary visit rather than relying on a supplement alone.

**Are pumpkin supplements safe to give alongside a senior dog's regular food?**
Generally, pumpkin and fiber toppers are designed to be mixed into meals, but every dog's tolerance for added fiber is different, especially in older dogs with other health conditions. Ask your vet before starting any new supplement, particularly if your dog is on medication or has an existing digestive condition.

**Do anal gland chews like Glandex replace the need for manual gland expression?**
That's a question for your veterinarian rather than something a supplement label can answer. Products marketing anal gland support, like the Vetnique Glandex chews, should be treated as a supportive addition, not a guaranteed substitute for veterinary care if scooting or gland issues persist.
