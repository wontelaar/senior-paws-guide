## How to Choose

- **Size and weight of your dog**: Small senior dogs may do well with a lightweight handheld option, while larger dogs might benefit from a dryer with stronger airflow and a longer hose.
- **Adjustable heat settings**: Look for multiple temperature options so you can select a setting that matches your dog's comfort, and follow the listing's instructions for use.
- **Adjustable airflow/speed**: Multiple speed settings let you start gentle and adjust as needed, which is useful for a dog that's sensitive to strong airflow.
- **Hose length and flexibility**: A longer or extendable hose makes it easier to reach different areas of your dog's body without repositioning them too much, which matters for a dog with limited mobility.
- **Weight and handling**: A lighter unit can be easier to hold steady during longer sessions, especially if you're supporting your dog with your other hand.
- **Nozzle attachments**: Multiple nozzles can help direct airflow to specific areas like the legs, belly, or face.
- **Noise considerations**: Some models are marketed with noise-reduction features, which may be worth factoring in for a dog that's easily startled.
- **Warranty and reviews**: A warranty period and review count can give you a sense of what other buyers experienced, though it's worth reading current listing details for the latest specifics.
- **Brush-and-dryer combos**: A combined tool can simplify grooming into fewer steps, which is handy for dogs that don't tolerate long sessions.
