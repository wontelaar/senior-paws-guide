## How to Choose

- **Your dog's weight and size**: Check the listed weight limit and dimensions against your dog's actual size, and when a listing suggests sizing by length and height, measure your dog rather than relying on weight alone.
- **Interior room to move**: Compare the 20 x 12 x 12 inch footprint of the Amazon Basics carrier with the 17 x 11 x 11 inch size of the Henkelion and Top tasta.
- **Carrying style**: Decide whether you want loop handles, a shoulder strap, or both, so you can carry the bag however suits your trip, whether that's a quick walk to the car or a longer haul with hands-free convenience.
- **Ventilation**: Mesh panels or windows on multiple sides help air move through the carrier, which matters if your dog will spend time in warmer rooms or vehicles.
- **Easy cleanup**: A removable, machine-washable pad makes it simple to keep the carrier fresh between trips.
- **Storage needs**: A collapsible frame is worth considering if you want to fold the carrier flat when it's not in use.
- **Structure and openings**: Think about whether you'd prefer a front opening, a top opening, or both, since this affects how easily your dog can get in and out.
- Always check the specific listing for exact weight limits, dimensions and included features before buying.

## FAQ

**How much weight can a soft-sided carrier like this hold?**
The Henkelion and Top tasta carriers are listed for pets up to 15 lbs. This guide doesn't list a weight for the Amazon Basics or collapsible carriers, so check their listings for the exact weight limit and dimensions before buying.

**Should I size a carrier by my dog's weight or their body measurements?**
Weight limits are a useful starting point, but some listings recommend measuring your dog's length and height as well, since body shape can vary a lot between breeds at the same weight. Checking both gives you a better sense of whether your dog will be able to turn around and lie down comfortably inside.

**Is a shoulder strap necessary, or are handles enough?**
Loop handles work fine for short carries, but a shoulder strap frees up your hands and can make longer walks or multi-stop errands easier on your arms.
