Getting an older or arthritic small dog to the vet, around the neighborhood, or on a short trip is a lot easier when they don't have to walk or jump on sore joints. A soft-sided carrier with a shoulder strap lets you keep your hands free while your dog rides at your hip in a padded, zip-up space of their own. The options below range from compact 17-inch bags to a roomier 20-inch carrier and a collapsible mesh design, so you can match the size and features to your dog and your routine.

## Which Carrier Fits Your Dog?

Soft-sided carriers are judged by one simple question: can your dog stand, turn, and lie down inside without being squeezed against the mesh? Here's a two-minute way to check.

**Step 1: Measure your dog.** With your dog standing naturally, measure from the base of the neck (shoulders) to the base of the tail for length, and from the floor to the top of the shoulders for height. Use inches.

**Step 2: Add a little room.** A comfortable soft carrier is usually a bit longer and taller than your dog's body, so they can shift position without pressing against the sides. Add roughly 2 to 4 inches to each measurement as a rough comfort margin.

**Step 3: Compare to the carrier's floor space.** Match your dog's length-plus-margin against the carrier's interior length, and check the stated weight limit matches your dog on a scale.

| Your dog's body length + margin | Fits a 17 in long carrier? | Fits a 20 in long carrier? |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 13 in | Yes | Yes |
| 14–17 in | Likely, check height/width too | Yes |
| 18–20 in | No | Likely, check height/width too |
| Over 20 in | No | No |

## How to Choose

- **Your dog's weight and size**: Check the listed weight limit and dimensions against your dog's actual size, and when a listing suggests sizing by length and height, measure your dog rather than relying on weight alone.
- **Interior room to move**: A dog who likes to shift positions or lie flat may appreciate the larger 20 x 12 x 12 inch footprint, while a smaller 17 x 11 x 11 inch carrier suits dogs who prefer a snugger, more den-like space.
- **Carrying style**: Decide whether you want loop handles, a shoulder strap, or both, so you can carry the bag however suits your trip, whether that's a quick walk to the car or a longer haul with hands-free convenience.
- **Ventilation**: Mesh panels or windows on multiple sides help air move through the carrier, which matters if your dog will spend time in warmer rooms or vehicles.
- **Easy cleanup**: A removable, machine-washable pad makes it simple to keep the carrier fresh between trips.
- **Storage needs**: A collapsible frame is worth considering if you want to fold the carrier flat when it's not in use.
- **Structure and openings**: Think about whether you'd prefer a front opening, a top opening, or both, since this affects how easily your dog can get in and out.
- Always check the specific listing for exact weight limits, dimensions, and included features before buying, and ask your vet if you have questions about carrying a dog with a health condition.

## FAQ

**How much weight can a soft-sided carrier like this hold?**
The carriers in this guide are listed for pets up to 15 lbs, which generally covers small dog breeds. Always check the specific listing for the exact weight limit and dimensions before buying to make sure it matches your dog.

**Should I size a carrier by my dog's weight or their body measurements?**
Weight limits are a useful starting point, but some listings recommend measuring your dog's length and height as well, since body shape can vary a lot between breeds at the same weight. Checking both gives you a better sense of whether your dog will be able to turn around and lie down comfortably inside.

**Is a shoulder strap necessary, or are handles enough?**
Loop handles work fine for short carries, but a shoulder strap frees up your hands and can make longer walks or multi-stop errands easier on your arms and on your dog, who may appreciate a steadier, less jostled ride.
