## What Size Toy Fits Your Dog?

A plush toy should be easy for your dog to grab, carry, and shake without feeling too small to find or too large to hold. Here's a quick way to match a toy to your dog in two minutes.

**Step 1: Measure your dog's muzzle width.** With your dog relaxed, measure across the bridge of the nose, just behind the nostrils. This tells you roughly how wide a toy their mouth can comfortably close around.

**Step 2: Note your dog's weight class.** Weigh your dog (or check a recent vet visit) and sort them into small, medium, or large, since most plush toys are listed by weight group rather than exact inches.

**Step 3: Match the toy length to the weight group.** As a simple guide, small dogs generally do well with toys in the 5 to 7 inch range, medium dogs with toys around 8 to 10 inches, and both can often share toys in the middle of that range.

| Dog weight group | Comfortable toy length range |
|---|---|
| Small (under 20 lb / 9.1 kg) | 5–7 in (12.7–17.8 cm) |
| Small to medium (20–40 lb / 9.1–18.1 kg) | 6–9 in (15.2–22.9 cm) |
| Medium (40–60 lb / 18.1–27.2 kg) | 8–10 in (20.3–25.4 cm) |

## How to Choose

- **Dog size:** Match the toy's size to your dog's size — small dogs tend to do well with compact plush shapes like the Bunny Buddy or Loofa, while medium dogs may enjoy larger options like the Lamb Chop or the fleece bone.
- **Squeaker setup:** Decide if your dog likes a single squeak or multiple sound points — the Lamb Chop's five squeakers offer more sound variety, while single-squeaker toys keep things simple.
- **Sound preference:** Some dogs respond better to a crinkle than a squeak, which is worth considering with toys like the Bunny Buddy that offer sound alternatives.
- **Shape and design:** Think about whether your dog prefers animal-shaped toys, like the rabbit or lamb, or simpler shapes like a bone or loofa-style toy.
- **Material details:** If you prefer a toy without plastic parts, the fleece bone's design without plastic eyes or buttons may stand out.
- **Chew style:** Light chewers and dogs who enjoy gentle carrying or shaking are generally well suited to soft plush toys; always supervise play to see how your dog interacts with a new toy.
- **Variety and color:** If color options matter, check the listing to see what's available, as some toys like the Loofa ship in an assorted color.

## FAQ

**Are plush squeaky toys suitable for all dogs?**
Plush toys are generally intended for gentle play, and supervision during playtime is always a good idea. Dogs with more intense chewing habits should be matched to toys designed with that in mind, so check the listing details for the intended use.

**How many squeakers should a plush toy have?**
It depends on what keeps your dog engaged — some dogs enjoy a single squeaker, while others respond well to multiple squeak points like the five found in the Lamb Chop toy. Trying different options can help you learn your dog's preference.

**Do these toys come in different colors or sound types?**
Some do. For example, the Loofa ships in an assorted color, and the Bunny Buddy is available with crinkle sound alternatives in addition to its squeaker. Checking each product listing will give you the most current options available.
