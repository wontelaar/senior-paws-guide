## What Size Pool Does Your Dog Need?

A pool that fits means your dog can stand and turn around with room to spare. Here is a quick way to size it up.

**Step 1: Measure your dog.** With your dog standing, measure from nose to base of tail (body length) and note their shoulder height. A tape measure or soft string works well.

**Step 2: Add turning room.** A comfortable pool diameter is roughly your dog's body length plus 12 to 18 inches, so they can stand, turn, and lie down without their paws hanging over the edge.

**Step 3: Check the depth against shoulder height.** For wading and cooling off rather than full submersion, a depth at or below the lower half of your dog's shoulder height is a comfortable match. Check with your vet first if your dog has joint or mobility concerns that affect getting in and out of a pool.

## How to Choose

- **Dog size and number of dogs**: A 32-inch pool suits a single small dog, while 48- to 63-inch pools give mid-size dogs more room, and the 67-inch option is suited to larger dogs or multiple pets using the pool together.
- **Depth**: These pools range from 8 to 12 inches deep, so consider how much water coverage you want for your dog's size.
- **Shape**: Round pools (48, 63, and 67 inch) offer a wide circular play area, while the 32-inch oval pool fits neatly into narrower spaces.
- **Material and bottom texture**: Vinyl and PVC construction with slip-resistant or anti-skid textured bottoms is common across these pools, which is worth noting if you want a consistent feel underfoot.
- **Drain style**: Some pools use a bottom or side drain, while the smallest model features a spiral water outlet — either way, check how each listing describes emptying the pool so cleanup fits your routine.
- **Storage space**: All four options fold down when not in use, so think about how much closet, garage, or shed space you have for storage.
- **Setup effort**: None of these pools require inflation, so setup mainly comes down to unfolding and filling with water.

## FAQ

**Do these pools need a pump or inflation?**
No. Each of the four pools listed here is designed to set up without an air pump or inflation — you simply unfold it and fill it with water.

**How do I drain the water when I'm done?**
It depends on the model. The Jasonwell and SLOOSH pools are listed with drains for emptying, the VISTOP pool is built with a hard plastic shell and anti-skid bottom, and the compact 32-inch pool uses a spiral water outlet. Check the specific listing for full draining instructions.

**Is a foldable dog pool a good option for an older or arthritic dog?**
Many owners choose shallow, easy-access pools like these for low-impact water play, though it's worth asking your vet if your dog has a specific health condition before introducing new activities.
