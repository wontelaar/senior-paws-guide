## How to Choose

- **Ease of putting it on**: Look for side-buckle closures, ponchos, or step-in designs that don't require lifting legs or flipping a stiff dog onto its back.
- **Coverage area**: Decide whether your dog needs back-only coverage, full four-leg coverage, or extra hip and thigh coverage, based on how they tend to get wet or muddy.
- **Leash compatibility**: If your senior dog already wears a harness, a coat with a leash portal or hidden leash hole means less undressing and redressing on walks.
- **Sizing**: Check size charts carefully, since a properly fitted coat will be more comfortable than one that's too snug around the shoulders or too loose to stay put.
- **Visibility features**: Reflective strips are a useful bonus for early morning or evening walks when light is low.
- **Weight of the fabric**: A lightweight shell can be easier for an older dog to tolerate than a heavier, stiffer material.
- **Adjustability**: Straps at the belly, chest, neck, or hood allow you to customize fit as your dog's coat, weight, or comfort needs change over time.

## FAQ

**Do senior dogs need a raincoat?**
Many owners of older dogs like having a waterproof layer for walks in wet weather, especially for dogs who are less tolerant of being handled or dried off afterward. If your dog has a health condition, it's worth asking your vet whether a specific coat style suits their mobility.

**How do I measure my dog for a raincoat?**
Check the specific sizing chart on each product listing, since sizing can vary between brands and styles like ponchos versus vest-style jackets.

**Can a raincoat be worn over a harness?**
Some designs, like those with a back leash portal, are made to work with a harness worn underneath, while others have their own built-in leash opening. Check each listing to see which setup fits your dog's existing gear.
