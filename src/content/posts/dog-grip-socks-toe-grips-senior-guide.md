Watching an older dog's paws splay out on the kitchen tile is unsettling, and it's a common enough problem that a whole category of grip socks and toe pads has popped up to help with traction on hard floors. This FAQ walks through the common questions people have before buying, including sizing, fit, and how these products compare to going sock-free.

### My senior dog keeps slipping on the hardwood floors — do grip socks actually fix that?

Grip socks and toe grip pads are designed to add traction between a dog's paw and a slick floor surface, which can help a dog feel more confident walking on tile, hardwood, or laminate. But it's worth being clear about what they are not: a sock or pad doesn't address why an older dog started slipping in the first place. New or worsening unsteadiness in a senior dog is often tied to arthritis, loss of muscle mass, nerve issues, or other age-related changes, and those deserve a vet visit rather than just a product purchase. Think of grip products as one piece of a broader plan for an older dog's mobility, not a stand-alone fix, and loop in your veterinarian if the slipping is new, sudden, or getting worse.

### Can I just use rugs or runners instead of buying socks?

Rugs, runners, and yoga-mat strips in high-traffic areas are a reasonable low-cost step and many households use them alongside grip socks rather than instead of them. The difference is coverage: a runner only helps in the spot where it's laid down, while socks or paw pads travel with the dog into the kitchen, down the hallway, or wherever the slick flooring is. If your dog's problem area is a single room, rugs might be enough on their own; if the whole house is hardwood or tile, many owners find it easier to pair some rugs in key spots with a paw-level solution like DOK TigerToes Premium Non-Slip Dog Socks <a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CN5WLZVL?tag=seniorpawsgui-20" target="_blank" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">DOK TigerToes Premium Non-Slip Dog Socks</a> for the areas rugs can't cover.

### How do I know what size to order?

Sizing for these products is based on paw width, not overall dog weight or breed, and it's measured while the dog is standing so the paw is naturally spread out rather than relaxed and compact. Measuring while standing matters because a paw measured while a dog is lying down can come out smaller than it actually is in use, leading to a sock that's too snug. ### Will these stop my dog from licking at their paws too?

Both EXPAWLORER Anti Slip Dog Socks <a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B089SWQQNX?tag=seniorpawsgui-20" target="_blank" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">EXPAWLORER Anti Slip Dog Socks</a> and the Double-Sided Grip Non-Slip Dog Socks for Senior Dogs <a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CW9RB4YR?tag=seniorpawsgui-20" target="_blank" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener">Double-Sided Grip Non-Slip Dog Socks for Senior Dogs</a> are marketed as helping discourage paw licking, presumably by putting a physical barrier between the tongue and the paw. That's a manufacturer claim rather than a guaranteed behavior fix, and licking in senior dogs can have a lot of underlying causes — allergies, dry skin, joint pain making a dog fixate on one leg, anxiety — that a sock won't resolve on its own. If the licking is frequent, intense, or focused on one particular spot, it's worth mentioning to your vet rather than assuming a sock will solve it.

### How snug should the socks be, and what should I watch for once they're on?

A sock that's too loose will slide around and lose whatever traction benefit it's supposed to offer, while one that's too tight can rub against the skin or restrict circulation, so getting the paw-width measurement right matters more than it might seem. Once a sock or pad is on, get in the habit of checking the paw and surrounding skin regularly for redness, swelling, or sore spots, and take the socks off periodically rather than leaving them on around the clock. If your dog seems distressed while wearing them, keeps trying to chew them off aggressively, or you notice any irritation on the skin, stop use and check with your vet before continuing.

Grip socks and toe pads can be a reasonable part of helping an older dog feel steadier on slippery floors, but they work best paired with attention to fit, regular paw checks, and a veterinarian's input on why the slipping started in the first place.
