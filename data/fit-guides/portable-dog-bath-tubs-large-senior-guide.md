## What Size and Weight Does Your Dog Need?

Match your dog's body length and weight to the tub's basin size and weight capacity before you buy, so your dog can stand and turn comfortably during a wash.

**Step 1: Measure your dog's length.** Measure from the base of the neck to the base of the tail, with your dog standing naturally. This tells you how much basin length they need to stand without crowding the ends.

**Step 2: Weigh your dog.** Use a bathroom scale (weigh yourself holding your dog, then subtract your own weight), and compare that number to each tub's listed weight capacity.

**Step 3: Check how your dog gets in.** Dogs who are senior, large, or not confident jumping benefit from stairs rather than lifting straight up and over a tub wall. If your dog has joint or mobility concerns, check with your vet before choosing a method of getting in and out.

**Basin length vs. dog length**

| Dog length (neck to tail) | Fits comfortably in a basin of |
|---|---|
| 18 in (46 cm) | 26 in or longer |
| 24 in (61 cm) | 32 in or longer |
| 30 in (76 cm) | 38 in or longer |
| 36 in (91 cm) | 43 in or longer |

**How to read it:** if your dog measures close to 36 inches from neck to tail, or is a large or senior dog who does better walking up than jumping in, the Furesh station's 43.1-inch basin and foldable stairs are built for that use. For dogs around 18 to 24 inches long, either the Beberoad's roughly 26-inch basin or the SereneLife's 360-degree access and adjustable height can work well, with the SereneLife adding a leash restraint for dogs who move around during a wash. The VEVOR lists a tub weight capacity up to 220 lb but is described for small and medium dogs and cats, so weigh your dog and confirm basin dimensions on the product page before relying on that number for a large breed.
