## What Size Does Your Dog (and Bed Space) Need?

Picking the right orthopedic bed is mostly about matching your dog's body length to the bed's sleeping surface. Here is a two-minute way to check it.

**Step 1: Measure your dog.** With your dog standing, measure from the tip of the nose to the base of the tail, then add the length of their legs if they like to stretch out fully when sleeping. Note the result in inches.

**Step 2: Measure your dog's weight.** Use a bathroom scale (weigh yourself holding your dog, then subtract your own weight) and round to the nearest pound.

**Step 3: Compare to the bed's listed footprint.** Check the bed's length and width against your dog's nose-to-tail measurement, leaving a few inches of margin so they can curl up or stretch without hanging off the edge.

**Bed dimensions at a glance**

| Bed length needed (nose to tail) | Comfortable margin added | Suggested bed length to look for |
|---|---|---|
| 28 in | +4 in | 32 in |
| 32 in | +4 in | 36 in |
| 36 in | +5 in | 41 in |

**How to read it:** if your dog measures closer to 36 inches nose to tail, the EHEYCIGA at 41 inches (also available in medium, large and extra-large sizes) gives the most room to stretch. For dogs listed in the 40 to 65 lb range, the Orthopedic Dog Bed 2-Layer at 36 x 27 inches is sized specifically for that weight bracket, and the OhGeni shares the same 36 x 27 inch footprint with a reversible foam layer. If your dog is on the smaller side of "large," at or under 50 lb, the KSIIA at 35 x 22 inches lines up with that listed limit. If your dog has joint sensitivity, it can help to check with your vet about what firmness or loft they recommend before choosing a foam type. As always, confirm exact dimensions on the product page before ordering.
