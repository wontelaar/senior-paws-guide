## Will It Fit Your Doorway, Stairway or Room?

Extra-wide gates only work for senior dogs if the width, height and mounting style actually suit the opening — and your dog's mobility. Here is a two-minute way to check before you buy.

**Step 1: Measure the opening.** Measure wall-to-wall (or banister-to-wall) at the widest point of the doorway, hallway, or stairway top. Note the width in inches.

**Step 2: Measure the height your dog could clear.** A taller gate matters more for dogs that still like to lean or try to climb. If your dog has joint or mobility concerns, check with your vet about a comfortable step-over height.

**Step 3: Decide how it needs to mount.** Pressure-mounted gates grip between two solid walls with no drilling. Hardware-mounted gates bolt in for stairs. Freestanding gates need no walls at all, which suits open rooms.

**What width range do you need?**

| Your opening | Fits which listed range |

**How to read it:** if your opening measures close to 60 inches and you have two solid walls, the Extra Wide Baby Gate or the Amibud both reach into that range, with the Amibud's 41-inch height suited to a dog that leans on gates. For a stairway, the Extra-Wide Auto-Close Gate adds hardware mounting and round-banister adapters. For an open room with no walls nearby, the PAWLAND's freestanding panels unfold up to 144 inches. Always confirm exact dimensions on the product page before ordering.
